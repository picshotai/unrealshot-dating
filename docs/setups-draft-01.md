# Setup drafts 01 — three shoots, twenty frames

Hand-written as complete units. No tokens, no substitution: the string below is
the string that would be sent.

**How to judge these.** The question is not whether one frame is good. It is
whether a *setup* holds together across its beats — same man, same clothes, same
place, same light, six believable moments. If that holds, the model scales. If it
does not, we stop here.

Design rules applied, from the Seedream findings:

- One clear action per frame. No simultaneous body actions.
- Face large. Nothing wider than three-quarter; no full-body distance shots.
- Light is either hard frontal flash or bright even daylight. No warm dim
  interiors, no moody low-light, no light source behind him.
- Pose stated as mechanics — which shoulder is lower, what the hands touch, where
  the head is relative to the torso — instead of "relaxed" or "candid".
- No text-bearing objects, no second person, no negation.

## What to look for when you run these

1. **Within a setup, does he look like the same man in the same clothes?** That
   is the whole test. Six frames should read as six minutes, not six days.
2. **Is the face large and sharp enough** at three-quarter distance, or does it
   start to drift the moment he is further than waist-up?
3. **Does the flash setup (1) beat the daylight setups (2, 3)?** Your findings say
   flash is a strength. If setup 1 wins clearly, the library should lean that way.
4. **Which beats feel posed?** The laugh beats and the hand-in-hair beats are the
   two most likely to come back stiff. If they do, they get cut from the pattern
   rather than repaired.
