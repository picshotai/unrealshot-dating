# Positioning Brief — Dating Shoot

**For:** whoever is writing landing page, pricing, email or in-app copy
**Status of numbers in this doc:** every figure was read out of the shipping code on 2026-08-20 and is listed with its source file in section 10. Do not round them up. Do not invent new ones.

**This brief replaces the version dated 2026-08-18.** That one was built on a
guarantee the product no longer makes — see section 2. If you are working from a
draft that says "100 photos, no two alike," it is out of date and the claim is
now false.

---

## 1. The one-sentence shift

**Stop selling a pile of individually different photos. Start selling fifteen photoshoots.**

Everything below is downstream of that sentence.

---

## 2. What changed, and why the old claim had to go

The previous position was: *100 photos, no two in the same outfit, no two in the
same light, 100 different places* — enforced in code, and genuinely the
strongest claim in the category.

It was built on an architecture that assembled every photo independently from a
location, a backdrop, an outfit and a light. That is what made "no two alike"
true, and it is also what made the photos bad: nothing ever checked whether the
combination made sense, and a navy topcoat and leather dress shoes ended up
running up a forest trail. **147 of 1,170 combinations were incoherent.** Live
testing returned 5 usable photos out of 20.

The replacement is the opposite idea. A **shoot** is one location, one outfit,
one light, and four frames of it — written by hand, read by a person, sent
verbatim. Inside a shoot the outfit repeats *on purpose*, and the frames are
generated against each other so the room, the clothes and the light hold
still.

So the no-repeat guarantee is dead, and it should not be quietly rewritten into
something vaguer. The honest replacement is better, and section 3 is the whole
argument for why.

---

## 3. Why "fifteen shoots" beats "sixty photos"

datephotos.ai and everyone like them sell on inventory counts — "40+ scene
variations," "each scene has 3-4 poses," "94+." We used to attack that by
pointing out the arithmetic: 40 scenes into 100 photos means repeats.

We now deliver 4 photos per shoot and say so on the pricing page. So the attack
has to change, and it changes into something truer.

**A profile made of sixty unrelated one-offs does not look like a person. It
looks like a stock library.** Sixty photos where the man is in a different city,
a different coat and a different season in every frame reads as generated,
because no real human being photographs their life that way. What a real
person's camera roll looks like is a handful of days, several photos from each.

That is exactly what we deliver, and it is the thing competitors structurally
cannot do — if you compose each photo independently, you can never produce two
photos that are convincingly of the same moment.

> **Fifteen shoots. Four photos from each. Every shoot a different place, a
> different outfit and a different light.**

Lead with that. The four-per-shoot number is a feature; put it in the headline
rather than burying it and hoping nobody divides.

---

## 4. Verified claims — this is the approved claim set

Use these freely. Each one is true of every delivery.

**Per delivery**

- 15 shoots, 60 photos
- Every shoot is a different place, a different outfit and a different light — no shoot repeats another
- 4 photos from each shoot: a close portrait, a half-body, a full-length and a candid
- One person in every frame: him. No filler crowds, no background extras.
- Photos arrive in the crops dating apps actually use — tall portrait and wide
- 30 free re-shoots of any individual photo he does not love
- Up to 3 shoots built around each thing he says he does, so one hobby never takes over the profile
- He can exclude 4 kinds of content: drinks in hand, dogs, bikes, team sport. A shoot featuring any of them is dropped whole.
- Setup needs 4–6 reference selfies

**Behind it (use for depth and credibility sections, not headlines)**

- Every prompt is hand-written and read by a person before it can be sent. Nothing is assembled at generation time.
- 72 authored frames in the library; a delivery draws 60 of them
- 18 distinct outfits, 18 distinct locations, 18 distinct lighting setups
- Each shoot's first frame is generated first, and the other three are generated *against it* — which is what holds the room, the clothes and the light still across the set

**The honest superlative, if you want one:** every other tool in this category
generates sixty unrelated pictures. We shoot fifteen sessions. That is the
difference between a folder of AI images and a profile that reads like a life.

## 5. The lineup still works — as a filter, not the structure

He is buying a profile already sorted into the slots a dating app asks him to
fill. Each shoot contributes one of each:

| Slot | What it is for | Which frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your opener** | The first photo. The one that decides the swipe. | the close portrait |
| **Your full body** | The one every app and every user asks for. | the full-length |
| **What you do** | Him doing the thing he actually does. | any frame from an activity shoot |
| **Out in the world** | Him somewhere real, living a life. | any frame from an outdoor or social shoot |
| **The rest** | Depth. The photos that make a profile read as a person. | everything else |

**The delivery is presented by shoot, and the slots are a filter over it.** That
is a change from the old page: the shoot is the hierarchy, because the shoot is
the product. Do not write copy that implies the set arrives sorted into five
folders — it arrives as fifteen shoots he can filter five ways.

Do not promise an exact photo count per slot.

---

## 6. What the app asks him — the page must set matching expectations

The intake asks exactly three things. Landing copy should promise nothing that
contradicts them:

1. **"Which look should we lead with?"** — three photos of wardrobes, not the words casual/sharp/street.
2. **"What do you actually do?"** — 22 interests, multi-select. No free text: every chip is backed by a real shoot, and a typed word that matches nothing could only ever be ignored.
3. **"Anything to leave out?"** — the four exclusions.

**Critical nuance on question 1.** He picks what to *lead with*, not what he
gets. His answer weights which shoots he receives; it never locks them. Sixty
photos in one register reads flat, so the range is the product. Phrase it as:
*every delivery covers the range; you choose where it leans.*

**Critical nuance on question 2.** An interest chip is now a real promise. Pick
climbing and you get shoots written around climbing — not a generic venue with
the word dropped into it, which is what the old system did. This is worth
saying out loud; it is a concrete, checkable improvement.

Everything else is automatic. That is a selling point — say so. He answers three
questions; he does not build a shot list.

---

## 7. Objections to pre-empt

- **"Will it look like me?"** — 4–6 selfies; his identity is held to one person across all 60. Do not overclaim perfection; we accept minor drift on a small number of frames.
- **"Will it look AI?"** — The camera and lighting specifications are the point: real focal lengths, real light directions, phone-plausible framing. No filler people in frames, which is where AI photos usually give themselves away.
- **"Aren't four photos from one shoot just the same photo?"** — No, and this is worth answering directly: the four differ in distance and in what he is doing, from a close portrait to a full-length to a candid. Same day, four photographs. That is what a real shoot produces.
- **"Only 60?"** — Sixty photographs he would actually post, rather than a hundred he has to sift. Do not get defensive; the smaller number is the confident one.
- **"What if I hate some?"** — 30 free re-shoots, and a re-shoot stays inside its shoot, so the replacement still matches its three siblings.

---

## 8. Do not claim any of these

Hard list. Several are things we cannot back, and a couple are things we
deliberately removed.

- **"No two alike," "no repeats," "100 different outfits," or any version of the old per-photo guarantee.** It is false now. A shoot repeats its outfit four times by design.
- Any internal category name — the five old buckets are gone from the code and must not reappear in copy
- "3 vibes, 3 styles" or any small combinatorial number
- Photos with friends, dates, groups, or people in the background — **we removed this on purpose.** The generated extras looked like mannequins and no man would post them. Never imply company in frame.
- Exact per-slot photo counts
- "Friend-taken," "shot by a friend," or any phrasing implying a second person held the camera
- Guaranteed match rates, date counts, or any dating-outcome promise
- Named comparisons to datephotos.ai or any competitor on the page
- Turnaround times or delivery-speed numbers — not verified; ask before writing any
- Anything about training a model on his face, LoRAs, or fine-tuning. That is not how this works.

---

## 9. Voice

Plain, specific, slightly blunt. He is a man who has been putting off dealing
with his photos and is a little embarrassed about it. He does not want to be
told he will "unlock his best self."

- Concrete over aspirational: "fifteen shoots, four photos from each" beats "unleash your best look."
- Numbers over adjectives. We have real numbers; adjectives read as cope.
- Never condescend about his current photos. Never imply he is failing at dating.
- Short sentences. No stacked superlatives. No emoji in headlines.

**Directions worth drafting against** (angles, not final copy — write better versions):

- **The camera-roll angle.** Real people have a few photos from a few days. That is what this delivers, and it is why it does not read as AI.
- **The finished-profile angle.** You do not get a folder. You get your profile, sorted.
- **The three-questions angle.** Answer three things; we handle the rest.
- **The hand-written angle.** Every prompt in this product was written and read by a person. Nobody else in the category can say that, and it is the reason the photos are coherent.
